## You bought the house, and everything in it

Buying a house is a lot. Inspections, closing, moving trucks, utilities, figuring out which switch turns on which light. The ductwork isn't usually on anybody's list.

But think about what's in there. Every year the previous owners lived in the house, the system moved their air around. If they had a dog or two, their pet hair went through those ducts. If they sanded floors or hung drywall before listing, some of that dust found its way into the vents. And if the house was flipped, there may have been a lot of construction dust moving through the system right before you got the keys.

None of that leaves with the moving truck. It sits in the ductwork until somebody cleans it out.

Here's how one customer put it after I cleaned her house:

> "Our home was built in '79 and has NEVER been cleaned!" Amanda T.

If nobody can tell you when the ducts were last cleaned, it's fair to assume they might never have been.

## Signs your new house's ducts need cleaning

You don't need to wait for a problem. But if you're not sure, here are the common signs I tell people to look for:

| Sign | What it can mean |
|---|---|
| Dust building up quickly after you've cleaned | Dust in the ductwork getting blown back into the rooms |
| Musty smell when the system runs | Something in the system that's worth a look |
| Weak airflow from the vents | Buildup or a problem in the ductwork |
| A recent remodel or flip before you bought | Construction dust from drywall or floor sanding in the ducts |
| You don't know when they were last cleaned | The most common one with a new house |

That last one covers almost every home buyer. Unless the seller handed you a receipt, you just don't know.

If you're seeing weak airflow from certain vents or uneven temperatures from room to room, mention it when you call. I'll look while I'm there, and if I spot damage, I'll show you photos, explain the issue and give you a price before anything gets done. I handle light repairs on the spot, like taping small holes or reattaching ductwork that's come loose. For major duct repairs, I'll refer you to another company.

## Don't forget the dryer vent

This is the one people really forget. You bring your own washer and dryer, hook it up, and start doing laundry. Nobody checks the vent line behind the wall.

Lint builds up in a dryer vent over time. When it's clogged, the dryer runs longer and hotter, and lint is highly flammable. Regular cleaning is one of the simplest ways to reduce fire risk. If you don't know when the last owner had it cleaned, it's worth doing before you start running loads every day.

A dryer vent cleaning covers the full vent line from the dryer to the outside, the transition duct behind the dryer, and the lint at the exterior opening. I check the airflow when I'm done, and you get before and after photos. It's a flat fee, and it usually takes under an hour.

If your dryer vent exits through the roof, call and tell me where it exits, and I'll confirm with you.

## When to schedule it

**If you're doing any work before you move in:** wait until the dusty work is finished. Drywall, floor sanding, that kind of thing. If I clean the ducts before that, the dust just ends up right back in them.

**If you're moving straight in:** sooner is better. You'll need to be home for access and a quick walkthrough when I'm done, and it helps to clear a path to the vents, the furnace and the dryer. That's usually easiest before every room is full of boxes.

**If you've already moved in:** that's fine too. It's not too late. Clear a path to the vents, furnace and dryer, secure the pets if you can, and I'll take it from there.

I can often come out the next day, depending on the schedule. You'll get a reminder with an arrival time before the appointment.

## Moving-in duct cleaning FAQs

**How do I know if my new house's ducts need cleaning?**
Common signs are dust building up quickly, musty smells when the system runs, weak airflow, a recent remodel, or not knowing when they were last cleaned. With a house you just bought, that last one usually applies.

**Should I clean the ducts before or after a remodel?**
After the dusty work, like drywall and floor sanding, is finished. Cleaning before that means the dust will just end up back in the ducts.

**Can you clean the dryer vent and air ducts in the same visit?**
Yes, and many customers do both at once.
